⏺ Fixed. The root cause:

web/middleware.ts does the CSP nonce setup correctly — generates a per-request nonce, attaches it to the request as x-nonce, and sets Content-Security-Policy on both the request and response. But for Next.js to actually stamp that nonce onto its framework inline scripts (hydration, streaming, RSC payload), the route has to be dynamically rendered.  Bubble visualization (SVG morphing path)

 Single <svg viewBox="-150 -150 300 300"> containing a <path> whose d
 attribute is rebuilt every animation frame.

 - 36 control points around a circle at base radius R = 110.
 - Each frame: pull FFT bins via getOutputByteFrequencyData(), split into
 low (bins 0–3), mid (4–15), high (16–31) bands, normalize to 0–1.
 - Per control point: blend the three bands by angle (lows widen the whole
 shape, mids ripple at 6 lobes, highs ripple at 12), plus a slow inline
 simplex-noise term for organic-ness.
 - Low-pass smooth radii frame-to-frame: smoothed[i] = smoothed[i] * 0.78 + 
 raw[i] * 0.22. Volume smoothing alpha 0.15.
 - Build d with closed quadratic Béziers between midpoints (cheap, smooth,
 no library).
 - Fill: <radialGradient> from #3d3a36 (basanite-700) at center to #c49a2f
 (gold-500) at 80%.
 - Glow: feGaussianBlur filter driven by a CSS variable --bubble-glow set
 per frame from getOutputVolume(). Wrapping <g> gets transform: scale(1 + 
 vol*0.04).
 - Idle (when !isSpeaking): deform = 6 (very small) + R += sin(t*0.0015)*4
 so the bubble breathes slowly.
 - Inline a ~40-line simplex-noise implementation rather than adding
 simplex-noise as a dep.

Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don't?
We have categorised our competitors into three groups and each has a different blind spot.

First, coding assessment platforms such as HackerRank and CodeSignal. They assess for standardised problems that tools like ChatGPT and Cluely solves trivially. They test pattern-matching on leaked question banks. The signal has collapsed.

Second, AI interview tools, examples are HireVue, Karat, and Mercor. These are automated versions of static structured interviews. Questions from the same question bank for every candidate, same leakability, just delivered faster. Mercor is closest to us but runs a generic interview not personalised to the candidate's experience, so it can't probe depth.

Third, traditional screens, for example Greenhouse and manual phone screens. They can't run consistent, deep first-round interviews at volume and requires senior engineers to lose hours on candidates who shouldn't be there.

What we understand: in a world where AI can answer any static question, the only defensible screen generates each interview from the candidate's actual experience and probes the reasoning behind their answers. Static question banks, however AI-delivered, are a dead instrument.
